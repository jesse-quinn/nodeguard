How to read these dashboards. nodeguard is an XDP firewall in the NIC driver, fed by a passive Suricata IDS. Everything fails open: an empty graph or a dead component means traffic flows unfiltered, never an outage. Every metric fails toward visible-unknown: a value that cannot be read shows as unsupported or no data, never as a reassuring zero. Each dashboard answers one 2am question, with every host on every panel.

Overview: is the firewall attached, enforcing, and healthy right now?

attach state: normal is "attached"; "detached" means no enforcement, and "unknown" means the tooling could not verify (assume still enforcing).

kill switch: normal is 0 (enforcing); 1 is soft-off, with the latch tile naming who set it (run nodeguard-on to re-arm).

prog match: normal is 1; 0 means the live program is not the one the tooling installed, which should never persist past a reload.

active blocks: entries currently in the block maps; normal is a slowly moving population that decays by TTL when the writers stop.

responder unit: the Suricata-alert-to-block daemon; normal is active.

anomaly count: cumulative volumetric-anomaly trips; normal is a flat line, and any increase deserves a look at the journal CRITICAL it left.

XDP drop rate (v4 and v6): packets per second dropped in-driver from blocklisted sources; nonzero means an active block is being exercised, and near-zero is normal between events.

XDP pass rate: internet traffic from globally routed sources surviving to the blocklist check; near zero on a NAT'd node is normal, it only sees its own return traffic.

Active blocks over time: block-map population; steps up on responder or feed activity and drifts down as entries expire in-kernel.

Suricata kernel drops (rate): capture ring overflow per second; normal is zero, and a sustained nonzero rate means reduced detection coverage, not a traffic problem.

Allowlist / WireGuard pass rates: traffic taking the never-blockable paths (private ranges, operator addresses, the tunnel port); normal is steady background matching your own use of the host.

Pass-path share (pie, per host): where passed packets exit the program (pass, allowlist, wgport, expired, nonip, parsefail); normal is dominated by plain pass, with expired and parsefail slivers.

Security: are we being scanned or attacked, and is the pipeline responding?

TCP sanity rates (stacked: SYN+FIN, SYN+RST, NULL, XMAS): impossible flag combinations counted on every packet that passes IP header validation; normal is zero to a trickle, and a step up means someone is scanning.

TTL-low and fragment counters: frames with implausibly low TTL or hop limit, plus v4 and v6 fragments; normal is a low background hum, and these count telemetry only, never a drop.

Alert-to-block correlation: suricata alert rate, responder would-block and blocks-issued, and XDP drop rate on one time axis; normal is the curves moving together, and divergence (alerts without blocks outside dry-run, or drops without alerts) is the story worth reading.

top blocked: the sources hitting the blocklist hardest since the last sweep walk (cumulative totals carried as a secondary figure); normal is a changing cast of strangers, and a familiar address here is a misconfiguration.

top1 hits: the single busiest blocked source's hit count; normal is modest, and a runaway number means one host is hammering you.

last alert / last action age: time since the responder last saw an alert and last acted; normal is minutes to hours, and both growing without bound means the detection path is quiet or broken.

watchdog canary failures: consecutive failures of the deliberately non-allowlisted probe; normal is 0, and 3 trips the over-block soft-off.

Capacity and Pipeline: will anything fill up or go stale before morning?

block-map utilization gauges (v4, v6): percent of map capacity in use per host; normal is well under the 70 warning and 85 alarm thresholds.

per-feed snapshot age (honeycomb): hours since each threat-intel feed last refreshed; normal is green (under 26 h), and red means the feed's entries are decaying toward no enforcement.

feeds entries: block-map entries owned by the feeds loader; normal is a stable count that steps on upstream publication days.

feeds candidates vs rejected vs failed: what each run fetched, dropped at the guards, and failed outright; normal is rejected and failed at zero.

sweep walk duration: how long the 10-minute map walk takes; normal is low and flat, and a rising trend means the maps are growing toward the point where the walk itself needs attention.

feeds enforce: normal is your intended mode; 0 is dry-run (diffs only, zero map writes).

feeds churn held: normal is 0; 1 means a feed changed composition beyond the churn brake and is waiting for operator review.

feeds journal reset: normal is 0; nonzero means the loader's ownership journal was rebuilt and deserves a look.

feeds map errors: normal is 0; nonzero means map writes failed mid-reconcile.

re-arm count: normal is 0; 1 means the watchdog used its once-per-boot automatic re-arm after a latch.

watchdog lifeline failures: consecutive cycles with every lifeline probe down; normal is 0, and 5 trips the outage soft-off.

sweep age: seconds since the sweep last wrote its snapshot; normal is under 600, and an alarm at 1800 means the counts on this page are going stale.
